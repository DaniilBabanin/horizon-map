Privacy notice

Last updated 8 November 2026

Who is responsible

Controller under the GDPR: Daniil Babanin. Postal address and contact: see the imprint.

In short

Horizon Finder runs entirely in your browser. It has no user accounts, no cookies, no analytics, no advertising and no server-side code of its own. Your browser does contact the services listed below to load the page and map data; they see your IP address and which files were requested.

Hosting: GitHub Pages and Cloudflare

The site's files (page, scripts, fonts, town and peak data) are hosted on GitHub Pages (GitHub, Inc., USA) and delivered through Cloudflare (Cloudflare, Inc., USA), which acts as a content delivery network and protects the site against attacks. When you open the site, these providers process your IP address, the time, the requested files, the referring page and your browser's user agent, and keep this in server logs for security and operation. Peak data are loaded in 2° × 2° tiles around the place you look at, so the requested file names reveal the approximate area.

Legal basis: Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR (legitimate interest in delivering the site securely and reliably). Both providers are based in the USA; where a provider is certified under the EU-U.S. Data Privacy Framework, transfers rely on the Commission's adequacy decision of 10 July 2023, otherwise on standard contractual clauses. Details: GitHub privacy statement, Cloudflare privacy policy.

Terrain tiles: Amazon Web Services

To compute what you can see, your browser downloads elevation tiles (Mapzen Terrain Tiles, part of the AWS Open Data programme) directly from Amazon S3 (Amazon Web Services, Inc., USA). Amazon receives your IP address and the tile addresses, which reveal the approximate area you are looking at. Without these tiles the tool cannot work. Legal basis: Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR. Transfers to the USA as described above. Details: AWS privacy notice.

Map overlay: OpenStreetMap (only when you switch it on)

The OpenStreetMap overlay is off by default. Only when you move its slider above 0 % does your browser load map tiles from tile.openstreetmap.org (OpenStreetMap Foundation, United Kingdom). The OpenStreetMap Foundation then receives your IP address, the tile addresses (the area shown), your user agent and this site's address as the referrer, which its tile usage policy requires. Legal basis: Art. 6(1)(a) GDPR (you switch it on) and Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR. Details: OSMF privacy policy.

Your device location

"Use my location" asks your browser for your position, and only after you click it; your browser asks for permission first. The position is used in your browser only and is never sent to this site. Placing the observer there does make your browser load the terrain and peak data for that area (see above), so those providers can infer the approximate area. Legal basis: Art. 6(1)(a) GDPR (your click and the browser permission, which you can withdraw in your browser settings at any time).

Stored in your browser

The site stores your last view and settings (position, heights, refraction, range, peak-name language, highlight colour, overlay strength) in your browser's local storage under the key horizon-v3, so the tool opens where you left it. This data never leaves your device. Storing it is strictly necessary for a function you asked for (§ 25(2) no. 2 TDDDG), so no consent banner is needed. You can delete it by clearing this site's data in your browser. The current view is also written into the address bar (after #); that part of the address is not sent to any server, but it is included when you share the link.

Your rights

You have the right to access (Art. 15 GDPR), rectification (Art. 16), erasure (Art. 17), restriction of processing (Art. 18), data portability (Art. 20) and to object to processing based on legitimate interests (Art. 21). Where processing is based on consent, you can withdraw it at any time with effect for the future. This site itself stores no personal data about you; for the providers' logs, contact them directly or write to the address in the imprint. You also have the right to lodge a complaint with a supervisory authority, for example Der Hessische Beauftragte für Datenschutz und Informationsfreiheit.

There is no automated decision-making or profiling.
